CertSmith 0.15.0 adds an experimental way to prove domain ownership without DNS credentials on the server:
a standing TXT record, published once (dns-persist-01). DNS-01 stays the default and nothing changes for
providers that use it.

## New

- **dns-persist-01 (experimental).** A certificate provider can validate names with one TXT record per domain,
  published once, instead of a temporary record on every issuance. Issuing and renewing then write nothing to
  DNS, and the provider needs no DNS credentials. It is for a CA that offers the challenge; it was developed and
  tested against Pebble, Let's Encrypt's test CA.
- **Check record.** A certificate on such a provider shows, for each name, the TXT record to publish (name and
  value, with Copy buttons) and whether it is in DNS: in place, not published, or not matching, with the reason.
- **An end date for the authorisation.** Optional, written into the record, with an alert 30 days before it.
- **Two new alerts.** *A dns-persist-01 record is missing or no longer matches* and *An authorisation is close
  to its end date*. Records are checked once a day, and each alert can be sent to email, the webhook or both.

## Improved

- **No pointless request to the CA.** With a standing record missing, issuance stops before the CA is asked and
  names the record, because a failed validation counts against the account's rate limits.
- **Fallback.** If the provider also has DNS credentials, a missing record, or a CA that does not offer the
  challenge, results in a DNS-01 issuance, and the log and events say so.

## Security

- With dns-persist-01 the ACME account key is the authorisation for the names: whoever holds it can obtain
  certificates until the record is removed or its end date passes. Keep backups of the data directory
  accordingly. The help page says the same.
